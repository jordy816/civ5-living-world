# Your first game

A walk through the first evening: what you see, what the game asks, and what is worth doing.
It follows one game from the table to the middle ages. The rules are Civilization V's, so
whatever you know from there holds; this page is about how it is played *here*.
[GAMEPLAY.md](GAMEPLAY.md) is the reference for everything named below.

The one thing to remember: **the big button at the bottom right always says what the game
needs next, and Enter presses it.** If you are lost, look there.

## Before the first turn

Start the instance. The world opens by itself and you sit at the table.

| 1. The game | 2. Who plays |
|---|---|
| ![The world with a sketch of its shape, and the rules](img/rts-lobby-game.png) | ![The civilizations, and who is at the table](img/rts-lobby.png) |

- **The land.** The sketch shows the shape of each kind of map. *Pangaea* (one continent) is
  the plain choice for a first game: everybody meets early and nobody needs ships. *Tiny* or
  *Small* keeps the turns of the other civilizations short.
- **The rules.** *Quick* is some 330 turns. *Prince* is even; below it you get help, above it
  the computer does. Leave the rest as it is.
- **Your civilization.** Point at one in the list: its gift in a few points, and what only
  its people train or build, with numbers. All of them are in
  [CIVILIZATIONS.md](CIVILIZATIONS.md). *Any* lets the game choose.
- **Begin the game.** A new world is made; that takes a moment.

## Turn 0: a Settler and a Warrior

You look down at a patch of land in the middle of cloud: what your people have seen. On it
stand your **Settler** (a wandering trader) and your **Warrior** (a zombie with an axe). The
first time, a page of mouse and keys comes up; **F1** brings it back.

The button says **UNIT NEEDS ORDERS**. Press **Tab** (or click the button): the view goes to
a unit that waits.

1. **Found your capital.** With the Settler selected, press **B**. The city stands where the
   Settler stood: a hall, a wall with towers in your colour, a banner with its name. The land
   around it is yours now: a wash of your colour with a band along the border.
2. **Send the Warrior out.** Select it and press **X**: it explores by itself and the cloud
   rolls back turn by turn. (Or right-click the land to send it somewhere yourself; the green
   outlines are how far it gets this turn.)

![Rome, just founded, seen from above](img/rts-city.png)

## The first choices

The button now says **CHOOSE PRODUCTION**. Click it: the city's list comes up on the left.

![What Rome can build](img/rts-production.png)

Every line says what the thing is, what it gives in numbers and small pictures (an apple is
food, a copper pickaxe production, a gold ingot gold, a bottle science, an amethyst culture)
and how many turns it takes. Point at a line for all of it. Click a line, or press its number.

A sound opening, as in Civilization V: a **Scout** to find the land and the ruins on it, a
**Monument** for culture, then a **Worker** or a second **Warrior**, then a **Settler** when
the city has grown to three or four.

Then **CHOOSE RESEARCH**. The list works the same way; **T** shows the whole tree, and a click
on any technology in it sets your scholars to work towards it by way of all it needs first.
Early on, research what your land asks for: *Animal Husbandry* shows horses and lets you
pasture sheep and cattle, *Mining* opens the hills and clears forest, *Pottery* brings the
Granary and the way to *Writing*.

![The tree of technologies](img/rts-techtree.png)

Now the button says **NEXT TURN**. Press **Enter**.

## What a turn is

When you end your turn the other civilizations move, the year goes on, and your cities grow
and build. The banner over a city counts down what it builds: *Worker 8*, then *7*, then *6*.

What happened comes as **small pictures down the right side**: a bell for a city, a pickaxe
for something built, a sword for war, a bottle for a discovery. A new one says its words for
a few seconds. **Click** one and the view goes to where it happened and opens what deals
with it; **right-click** puts it away.

![News as small pictures down the right side](img/rts-news.png)

A quiet stretch of the game is: **Enter**, read the button, a number key for whatever it
asks, **Enter** again.

## The first twenty turns

- **Your Scout or Warrior finds ruins** (a gift: gold, a technology, a map), **city-states**
  (small neutral cities: gifts of gold make friends of them) and **barbarian camps**.
- **Barbarians come.** They are illagers, and their units have a red outline when you can
  attack them. **Rest the pointer on one before you attack**: a card says who is the
  stronger and what each side is expected to lose. Right-click attacks. A unit that is hurt
  heals when it stands still; **F** fortifies it. A city with an enemy in reach has a small
  fire mark by its banner: click it, then a marked hex, and the city fires.

  ![A fight foretold](img/rts-battle.png)

- **Your Worker** improves the land: select it and press **Z**, and it gets on by itself
  (farms on the plains, mines in the hills, a pasture on the sheep). **Y** shows what every
  hex yields.
- **Your city grows.** Click it, then *Details*: what it makes a turn, three bars with the
  turns left until it grows, finishes what it builds and widens its border, and what stands
  in it.

  ![A city as a sheet of numbers and bars](img/rts-city-sheet.png)

- **Culture adds up**, and the button says **ADOPT A POLICY**. The policies are trees: gold
  squares can be chosen now. *Tradition* suits a few large cities, *Liberty* many small ones,
  *Honor* a people at war.

  ![The social policies, as trees](img/rts-policies.png)

- **A second city.** When the capital is three or four strong, build a Settler, walk it to a
  good spot a few hexes off (a river, a hill, a resource you lack) and press **B**.

## Meeting the others

Sooner or later a unit of another colour walks up. Click it, or a city of theirs, to speak to
its people: who leads them, how they look upon you, and what you can say. A **deal** is a
table: what you give on the left, what you get on the right, and at the foot what they think
of it, green when they would agree. A declaration of friendship is worth having early, and a
luxury you have twice is worth gold to somebody who has none.

| Speaking to another people | A deal on the table |
|---|---|
| ![Whom you speak to, what they say, what you can say back](img/rts-page-civ.png) | ![What you give, what you get, and what they think of it](img/rts-deal.png) |

## Go down and walk it (F12)

Point at a hex of your own land and press **F12**: you stand there in your own person, in
leather in your colours with a wooden sword. F12 again lifts you back up. Your figure stays
where you walked, marked *YOU* on the land and the small map.

Early in a game this is worth more than it looks:

- **Fell three trees on your own land** and carry the logs to your city (right-click the
  governor in the hall, *Deliver what you carry*): 2 Production a log. A city takes at least
  6 a turn from your hands, and a young capital makes only four or five itself: for the
  first turns you can more than double what it builds with. What you take grows back every
  turn.
- **Wheat, fruit and fish** are Food (a city takes 2 and one for every citizen a turn), **gold
  and gems** are Gold (10 a turn).
- **The bell in the hall** ends the turn, so you can stay down for a while.

Your hands work your own land only: beyond your borders nothing breaks and nothing is set
down.

![On your own land: the corner of the screen says whose land you stand on](img/foot-your-land.png)

**An enemy unit you come near sends its soldiers at you**: up to three, one after the other,
life-size and with a will of their own. Each one you beat costs that unit 20 health in the
game. With the wooden sword and leather you start with, three soldiers of a Warrior are a
close fight; a governor sells better arms (*Arms for yourself*) as soon as your people know
how to make them, and training for a heart more. If you fall you stand before your capital
again, a tenth of your treasury poorer.

![A soldier beaten, and the next on his way](img/foot-soldiers.png)

## Into the middle ages

- **Faith.** A shrine or a wonder brings faith; with enough of it the button says **CHOOSE A
  BELIEF** and you found a pantheon, later a religion (**R**).
- **Wonders.** A wonder of the world is not a line in a list here: it stands on a hex of its
  own beside its city, many times the size of a house. Others can see what you built.

  ![Wonders around Rome](img/rts-wonders.png)

- **The ages.** With every age your cities are built anew in its manner: thatch and timber,
  then columns and tiles, then stone keeps and half-timbered houses.
- **War, if you want it.** Units are Minecraft's creatures, the stronger the larger:
  [UNITS.md](UNITS.md) says who is who. Bring three or four units and something that shoots
  to a city; when its health is gone, the soldier you send into it takes it.
- **Ways to win** (**V**): science, culture, domination, diplomacy, or the score when time
  runs out. The page says how far you and the others are.

![Rome at turn 60](img/rts-turn60.png)

The game is saved after every turn. Leave when you like; next time it is there behind
*Continue*.

## The keys you will use most

| | |
|---|---|
| **Enter** | The turn button, whatever it says |
| **Tab** | The next unit that waits |
| **B**, **F**, **Space** | Found a city, fortify, skip this turn |
| **Z**, **X** | Let the unit get on by itself, or explore |
| **1** to **9** | A line of the list that is up |
| **T**, **P**, **V**, **R** | Technologies, policies, ways to win, religion |
| **Y**, **M**, **H** | Yields on the land, the known world, home to your capital |
| **N** | All the news so far |
| **F12** | Down to the land where you point, and back up |
| **F1** | Every key and what the mouse does |
